ESP32-C3 SuperMini wiring

5 V3.3 V GNDdigital analogI2C speaker drive

The board is drawn as it looks from the top with the USB-C port up, and the pin numbers match what is printed on it. A rounded tag such as 5V, 3.3V or GND means "connect to that pin on the right header" (printed 5V, 3.3, G). Every GND is joined together.

Two rules that protect the board

1. Never put 5 V into a GPIO. The HC-SR04's ECHO pin outputs 5 V, so it goes through the 1 kΩ + 2 kΩ divider. Power the joystick, ADXL345 and OLED from 3.3, not 5V.

2. Never wire the speaker straight to a GPIO. A 4–8 Ω speaker would pull about 400 mA and burn the pin, which can supply about 40 mA. The transistor does the heavy lifting.

Parts beyond what you have

  • Resistors: 1 kΩ ×2, 2 kΩ ×1 (or two 1 kΩ in series), 22 Ω ×1. A 10 kΩ + 20 kΩ pair also works for the divider.
  • NPN transistor: 2N2222, S8050 or PN2222.
  • Diode: 1N4148 or 1N4007.
  • Capacitor: 100 µF (any voltage of 10 V or more).
  • Easier speaker option: a PAM8403 amp module (about $1). Wire GPIO4→L-IN, G→GND, 5V→VCC, speaker→L+/L−. Then skip the transistor parts.
  • No resistors for the divider? Some newer HC-SR04 boards (HC-SR04P, RCWL-9610) run on 3.3 V. Power them from 3.3 and connect ECHO directly.
  • Power: a USB power bank into the SuperMini's USB-C. Its 5V pin then feeds the HC-SR04 and the speaker.

Bring-up order

  • Flash with USB CDC On Boot: Enabled and open the Serial Monitor at 115200.
  • Wire and test one part at a time: speaker, then ultrasonic, joystick, ADXL345, OLED. Use firmware/hardware_test: type one letter in the Serial Monitor to test each part.
  • Set WIFI_SSID, WIFI_PASS and BACKEND_HOST. The blue on-board LED lights when the cane reaches the server.
  • Fall test: lay the cane flat on the floor with a thump. The siren should start after about 2 s, then the SOS goes out 5 s later. Pressing or moving the joystick, or picking the cane up, cancels it.
